# Marketing Mix Modeling: Data Readiness & Decision Brief

Use this framework to assess whether marketing and business data can support a responsible marketing-mix analysis, then communicate what the model can and cannot say.

## Use it when

A team is considering a marketing-mix model, comparing a model output with an experiment, or preparing a budget decision that depends on modeled channel contribution.

## Prepare

Dated outcome series; media exposure and spend by channel and time; geography where relevant; pricing, promotions, distribution and other business controls; known tracking or taxonomy changes; experiment evidence; data owner and decision horizon.

## Workflow

1. Name the decision, geography, time unit, outcome and channels before selecting a model.
2. Inventory data coverage, definitions, missing periods, granularity, spend/exposure consistency and structural breaks. Document the reason for each exclusion or transformation.
3. Check whether the dataset contains the channel and business variation needed for the intended question; do not treat a long time series alone as proof of identifiability.
4. Record priors, controls, carryover/saturation choices, validation design and any experiment calibration as assumptions to review.
5. Report estimates with uncertainty and sensitivity; distinguish attributed performance, modeled contribution and experimentally estimated incrementality.
6. Translate findings into a decision range and a follow-up measurement plan. Revisit the model when data, channel mix or business conditions change.

## Included

A data dictionary, readiness checklist, transformation log, model-assumption register, validation plan, and decision-disclosure template. It is a planning and review framework, not a fitted model or budget recommendation.

## Review boundaries

Model quality depends on data, design and assumptions. Use current official method documentation and a qualified measurement reviewer; do not present modeled estimates as causal proof or guaranteed outcomes.
