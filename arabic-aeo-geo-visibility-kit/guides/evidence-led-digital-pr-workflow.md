# Evidence-Led Digital PR Workflow

This workflow helps a team turn a substantiated business insight into a useful editorial asset and a relevant journalist or publisher pitch. It is a content and outreach process, not a backlink scheme or a promise of search ranking, referral traffic, or AI citation.

## 1. Choose a defensible story

Start with original evidence that can be shared: a documented analysis, a useful tool, a transparent dataset, a first-hand operational insight, or a subject-matter expert who can answer a specific question. Define what the evidence can and cannot support before writing the headline.

Record:

- The audience question and why the story is useful to them.
- The underlying source, collection period, sample, method, limitations, and permission to publish.
- The claim each chart or sentence supports; remove claims the evidence cannot establish.
- The expert/author and accurate credentials relevant to the subject.
- Any commercial relationship, sponsorship, or material interest that readers should know.

## 2. Build the asset

Publish a clear landing page with an accurate title, descriptive headings, readable explanation, source notes, methodology, date, author, and accessible chart/table alternatives. Make the key finding understandable without requiring a journalist to infer it from an image. Keep the page useful even if no one links to it.

Google's guidance emphasizes helpful, original, people-first content and makes no promise that eligible content will be indexed or surfaced. Follow the current [Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) and [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content); do not create an asset solely to manufacture links or AI citations.

## 3. Match the pitch to the publication

- Identify a journalist/editor whose recent work actually covers the topic and audience.
- Use a short, accurate subject line and explain why the evidence is relevant to that person's coverage.
- State the finding, method/sample, limitation, and link to the source material without exaggeration.
- Offer an interview or additional context only when an available expert can provide it.
- Respect the publication's submission instructions, privacy expectations, and opt-out/contact preferences.
- Record the source and date used to verify any claim about a publication, journalist, or outreach service. Do not repeat stale claims about a platform's availability or pricing.

## 4. Track outcomes without overstating them

Record pitches sent, replies, resulting coverage, accurate mentions, links, referral sessions, and relevant conversions separately. Preserve URL, date, campaign tag where appropriate, and measurement limitations. A mention or backlink is not proof that PR caused a ranking change or that an AI engine will cite the page.

## 5. Close the loop

Correct factual errors through the publisher's normal process, update the asset when the underlying evidence changes, and note permission or retention limits. Use the observed response to improve relevance and clarity rather than increasing volume indiscriminately.
