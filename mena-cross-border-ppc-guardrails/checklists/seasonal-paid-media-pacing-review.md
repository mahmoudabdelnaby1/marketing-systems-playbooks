# Seasonal paid-media pacing review

Use this with Cross-Market Paid Media Planning when a campaign has a defined seasonal flight. The workflow uses the account's own history and current economics; it contains no assumed CPM uplift, universal daypart, or recommended budget percentage.

## Establish the plan

- [ ] Record market, currency, dates, objective, conversion definition, audience, placements, campaign owner, and approval status.
- [ ] Export a comparable account baseline by platform, market, objective, placement, and period. Record timezone, attribution window, and any material tracking changes.
- [ ] Record the approved total budget, daily/phase limits, cash-flow constraints, contribution margin, inventory, fulfilment capacity, and allowable acquisition cost.
- [ ] Build a dated pacing sheet with planned spend, actual spend, delivery, conversions, value, and notes by day/phase. Keep planned assumptions separate from observed results.
- [ ] If modeling scenarios, state the math and inputs. Do not paste in third-party seasonal cost multipliers as a forecast.

## Monitor and decide

- [ ] Compare cumulative actual spend with the approved plan and available delivery; distinguish pacing variance from performance variance.
- [ ] Check tracking, destination, offer, inventory, approval, and operational readiness before interpreting a change in cost or volume.
- [ ] Apply pre-agreed decision rules using account-specific limits. Document who can change budget, bids, targeting, or schedule.
- [ ] Test time-of-day or day-of-week changes only when data volume and business operations support a meaningful comparison; do not infer a universal peak from anecdotes.
- [ ] Pause or cap delivery when the offer, stock, tracking, policy approval, or fulfilment condition fails the agreed guardrail.

## Review

- [ ] Reconcile platform-attributed activity against CRM/store/finance records using declared definitions.
- [ ] Record actuals, variance, explanations, unresolved questions, and next actions. Do not describe correlation as causal lift.
