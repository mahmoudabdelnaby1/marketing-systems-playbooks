# Seasonal and cultural copy review

Use this checklist for Ramadan, Eid, national occasions, and other culturally specific campaigns. It is an editorial review aid, not a rulebook for how every person or market behaves.

## Brief and evidence

- [ ] Name the market, audience, language/register, channel, placement, offer, and campaign dates.
- [ ] State the audience need and the job of this message. Identify whether the campaign is informational, service-related, promotional, or a mix.
- [ ] Link every product, price, stock, delivery, eligibility, donation, and performance statement to an approved source and owner.
- [ ] Separate verified audience research from internal assumptions. Do not use population-wide or religious-behaviour claims without a current, relevant source.
- [ ] Confirm the exact dates and event references with the local campaign owner; do not treat calendar estimates as final where local observation or authority confirmation is relevant.

## Language and creative review

- [ ] Write for the specified audience and brand voice; do not treat Arabic dialects or Gulf markets as interchangeable.
- [ ] Ask a fluent local reviewer to check meaning, idiom, register, spelling, names, religious references, and unintended double meanings.
- [ ] Check that generosity, family, charity, or worship is not used as pressure, guilt, or an unsupported brand virtue claim.
- [ ] Check that the offer, terms, exclusions, dates, and CTA are as clear in Arabic as they are in English.
- [ ] Review the actual creative, crop, captions, sound, landing page, and placement together. Copy approval alone does not approve the full asset.

## Release and learning

- [ ] Confirm approvals, usage rights, destination page, stock/service capacity, customer-support coverage, and the owner for corrections.
- [ ] Preview every language and placement on a representative device; check RTL punctuation and mixed Arabic/Latin text.
- [ ] Record the final asset ID, reviewer, source links, approval time, and any unresolved limitation.
- [ ] After launch, compare like-for-like audience, placement, period, and objective. Treat response as campaign evidence, not proof that a cultural assumption was universally true.
